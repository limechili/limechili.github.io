1c. 其子不智。
Their son is not wise.

> 智 being a verb, 不 is used for verbs specifically.

其家甚智其子。
His family takes him to be extremely wise.

> This one confused me a lot. The textbook describes that verbs that don't take direct objects as "intransitive". When used transitively, the verb takes on a causative role. I like to think of it as "the act of {verb} is being applied to {object}." Thus 智其子 would mean that the act of being wise is being applied to his son, so then the family (其家) must truly believe he is wise, thus the translation of "his family takes him to be wise."


1d. 其財之亡大。
Their wealth, it departs greatly.

> 之 as a particle means either "'s" (possessive), "it", or "this". [Link to source](http://web.archive.org/web/20240412215137/https://eastasiastudent.net/china/classical/four-uses-of-zhi/)

大亡其財。
Their wealth greatly departs.

> Or based on the previous example, my literal translation would be "The act of departing is applied to the wealth."

### Questions:

1\. Second part is an unrelated comment giving more specificity to the scene being set by the first part. The third part is more about what is happening in the scene, and the fourth part is a consequence of the third part.

2\. The two parts of the second sentence: the first part is a consequence of the first sentence, and second part is a consequence of the first part.

3\. I would say that the topic in the fourth sentence is "今欲以先王之政治當世之民...". Based on the translation [see exercise 4 below], we can easily separate the sentence into "X are Y". Y is a comment on the topic X, thus separating at the "are" should split the topic and comment.

# Lesson 4

### Questions

1\. The function of 之 in sentence 3 is possessive. Describes that the sword's place of falling is X.

2\. The relation of 入水 to 求之 in sentence 4 is causal: "I 入水 in order to 求之".

3\. The function of 而 in sentence 5 is same as "but", indicates that there is a clear difference between the two parts.

4\. To me, the larger point of this parable is that the river crosser is narrowminded. Personally, I would not agree with the description "固執不知變通" (stubborn, not smart, not [situationally] flexible). More like, not making use of all the information in the big picture. It is correct that the sword fell at that spot from the boat point-of-view, but the river crosser did not take into account the movement of the boat.
